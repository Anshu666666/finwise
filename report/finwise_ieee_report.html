Executive Summary
Automated financial advisory and conversational wealth management systems have emerged as critical drivers for democratizing retail investing. However, deploying unconstrained generative Large Language Models (LLMs) in fiduciary environments introduces catastrophic systemic hazards: mathematical hallucinations, non-deterministic portfolio allocations, and violation of conservation laws (such as asset weights failing to sum to 100%). Conversely, legacy commercial robo-advisors rely on rigid step-function heuristics (e.g., the naive "100 minus age" rule or discrete boundary thresholds) that suffer from severe boundary cliff-effects—arbitrarily altering an investor's equity exposure overnight upon crossing a birthday or salary bracket.

To resolve this foundational trade-off between conversational flexibility and fiduciary safety, we engineered FinWise AI, an explainable neuro-symbolic financial advisory architecture. FinWise AI decouples subjective risk perception from deterministic constraint verification and natural language synthesis across three autonomous, coupled tiers:
  1. Tier 1: Continuous Mamdani Fuzzy Inference System (FIS) that utilizes continuous triangular and trapezoidal membership functions across four dimensions (Age, Horizon, Loss Tolerance, Income Stability) with numerical Center-of-Gravity (Centroid) defuzzification to quantify client risk appetite without artificial step-jumps.
  2. Tier 2: Priority Forward-Chaining Rule Engine that enforces non-negotiable statutory invariants ($\sum w_i = 100.0\%$, 6-month liquid emergency reserves, and high-interest debt avalanche scheduling).
  3. Tier 3: Grounded Agentic AI Layer (built on PydanticAI) that grounds conversational advice in live macroeconomic indicators (RBI Repo Rate, 10Y Sovereign G-Sec Yield) with a zero-hallucination deterministic fallback engine.
The architecture was rigorously validated across a 1,296-point 4D state-space grid (98.61% Monotonicity Concordance), a 50,000-profile Monte Carlo stress test (100% invariant preservation), a 150-case Certified Financial Planner (CFP) benchmark comparison (97.33% concordance), and an authentic 17-year historical market backtest (2008–2024). Over the 17-year historical backtest, FinWise Moderate achieved a Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) of 10.18% with an annualized volatility of 12.19% and a Sharpe Ratio of 0.302 (vs. 0.123 for pure Nifty 50 equity), cutting the maximum drawdown during the 2008 Lehman collapse from -51.80% down to -17.44%.

Chapter 1: Introduction & Problem Formulation

1.1. Background & The Growth of Automated Advisory

Retail financial participation in emerging economies has expanded exponentially over the past decade, driven by mobile smartphone penetration, low-cost discount brokerages, and digital payment infrastructure [1], [2]. In India alone, active mutual fund folios surpassed 190 million by 2024, with monthly Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) contributions exceeding ₹20,000 crores. However, the ratio of certified fiduciary investment advisers to retail investors remains critically inadequate—approximately one SEBI-registered investment adviser per 100,000 investors. Consequently, millions of novice investors allocate capital without personalized guidance, often chasing unvetted past performance or suffering catastrophic losses during inevitable market corrections.

1.2. The Fiduciary Dilemma: Why Raw LLMs Cannot Be Trusted

The advent of generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) and Large Language Models (LLMs) has sparked intense interest in conversational financial advisory interfaces [5]. While LLMs excel at synthesising conversational explanations and simplifying financial jargon, deploying unconstrained autoregressive language models in fiduciary roles introduces severe systemic hazards [6]:

  • Probabilistic Numerical Hallucination: LLMs operate on token probability distributions ($P(w_t \mid w_{[6]) demonstrate that commercial LLMs frequently recommend asset allocations where portfolio weights sum to 115% or 85%, or recommend negative cash positions for novice investors.
  • Violation of Fundamental Invariants: Language models cannot natively guarantee physical or mathematical conservation laws. In a fiduciary context, an asset allocation engine must guarantee $\sum_{i=1}^n w_i = 100.0\%$ under all circumstances.
  • Temporal Instability & Divergence: A client asking the exact same risk question across different conversational turns may receive contradictory advice due to temperature sampling and stochastic next-token generation.

1.3. The Failure of Discrete Scoring Heuristics (Cliff-Effects)

To circumvent LLM unreliability, legacy commercial robo-advisors historically adopted rigid questionnaire scoring matrices or naive rules of thumb, such as the static "100 minus age" equity formula. These systems assign discrete integer weights to multiple-choice answers and sum them into arbitrary risk brackets (e.g., Score 0–15 = Conservative, 16–30 = Moderate, 31+ = Aggressive).

This approach introduces destructive step-function discontinuities ("cliffs"). An investor turning 36 years old may find their recommended equity allocation abruptly slashed by 10% overnight, despite zero change in their financial horizon, salary, or psychological risk appetite. Such artificial boundaries distort optimal portfolio compounding and erode client trust.

1.4. Regulatory Compliance & The Fiduciary Standard

Under the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) Investment Advisers Regulations (2013) [10] and equivalent global standards (such as the US SEC Fiduciary Duty), automated advisers are legally obligated to act in the client's best interest. Fiduciary compliance requires:

  1. Explainability: Every recommendation must have a traceable, deterministic rationale that can be audited by human regulators.
  2. Suitability & Capital Preservation: Advice must account for multi-dimensional capacity (emergency reserves, high-interest debt, income stability), rather than evaluating market risk in isolation.
  3. Absolute Consistency: Identical client risk profiles must yield mathematically identical asset allocations.

Chapter 2: Literature Review & Theoretical Foundations

2.1. Modern Portfolio Theory (MPT) & Lifecycle Allocation

Modern Portfolio Theory, established by Harry Markowitz (1952) [3], proves that an asset's risk and return should not be assessed in isolation, but by how it contributes to an overall multi-asset portfolio. By combining assets with low or negative covariance (such as domestic equities, sovereign debt, and physical gold), investors can construct an "efficient frontier" offering the highest expected return for a given level of risk:

$$\sigma_p^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n w_i^2 \sigma_i^2 + 2 \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j < i} w_i w_j \text{Cov}(R_i, R_j)$$

William Sharpe (1964) [4] extended MPT through the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM) and formulated the Sharpe Ratio ($S_p = (R_p - R_f) / \sigma_p$), quantifying risk-adjusted excess returns over the risk-free rate ($R_f$). However, classical mean-variance optimization suffers from severe input sensitivity: minor estimation errors in expected return vectors lead to extreme, un-investable portfolio weights. Consequently, institutional wealth managers utilize structured asset allocation frameworks that enforce practical diversification bounds.

2.2. Fuzzy Set Theory & Linguistic Risk Modeling

Lotfi Zadeh (1965) [8] introduced Fuzzy Set Theory to model real-world systems characterized by vagueness and ambiguity rather than crisp binary classification. In classical Aristotelian logic, an element $x$ belongs to a set $A$ with a binary indicator $\chi_A(x) \in \{0, 1\}$. In fuzzy logic, membership is characterized by a continuous membership function $\mu_A(x) \in [0, 1]$, representing the degree of truth.

Ebrahim Mamdani and S. Assilian (1974) [9] designed the Mamdani Fuzzy Inference System (FIS), which models expert knowledge using intuitive linguistic IF-THEN rules. Mamdani FIS is uniquely suited for human wealth advisory: human perceptions of "risk", "young age", or "stable income" are continuous and overlapping rather than rigid binary numbers. By processing continuous inputs through triangular and trapezoidal membership functions, Mamdani FIS provides smooth, Lipschitz-continuous risk scores that eliminate cliff-edge artifacts entirely.

2.3. Production Rule Engines & Deterministic Invariant Systems

Rule-based expert systems utilize formal forward-chaining deduction ($P \implies Q$) to infer conclusions from verified facts. In safety-critical architectures, priority-queued rule engines guarantee that non-negotiable constraints (invariants) execute deterministically before unconstrained optimization algorithms. In FinWise AI, forward-chaining rules serve as an immutable mathematical firewall, guaranteeing that client portfolios never violate basic fiduciary mandates regardless of conversational inputs.

Chapter 3: End-to-End System Architecture

3.1. The Three-Tier Neuro-Symbolic Pipeline

FinWise AI is designed as a hybrid neuro-symbolic system that partitions financial decision-making into three specialized layers:

  1. Tier 1: Continuous Mamdani Fuzzy Inference Engine — Responsible for psychological risk perception. It ingests 4 continuous investor attributes and outputs a defuzzified crisp risk score $R^* \in [0, 100]$ via numerical Centroid integration.
  2. Tier 2: Priority Forward-Chaining Rule Engine — Responsible for fiduciary safety. It converts $R^*$ into a target equity allocation and applies a priority queue of production rules ($P \in [70, 100]$) to enforce emergency liquidity, debt elimination, 50/30/20 budgeting, and statutory tax planning.
  3. Tier 3: Grounded Agentic AI Layer — Responsible for conversational interaction. Powered by PydanticAI, it generates natural language explanations grounded in live macroeconomic indicators (RBI Repo rate, 10Y G-Sec yield) with a zero-hallucination deterministic fallback engine.

Chapter 4: Tier 1 – Continuous Mamdani Fuzzy Inference Engine

4.1. Mathematical Formulation & Membership Functions

The risk assessment engine maps four continuous investor inputs into linguistic terms: Age ($x_{\text{age}} \in [18, 85]$), Investment Horizon ($x_{\text{horiz}} \in [0, 30]$ years), Psychological Loss Tolerance ($x_{\text{loss}} \in [0, 100]$), and Income Stability ($x_{\text{inc}} \in [0, 100]$).

We implement piecewise triangular ($\mu_{\text{tri}}$) and trapezoidal ($\mu_{\text{trap}}$) membership functions, formulated as follows:

$$\mu_{\text{tri}}(x; a, b, c) = \max\left(0, \min\left(\frac{x-a}{b-a}, \frac{c-x}{c-b}\right)\right)$$
(1)
$$\mu_{\text{trap}}(x; a, b, c, d) = \max\left(0, \min\left(\frac{x-a}{b-a}, 1, \frac{d-x}{d-c}\right)\right)$$
(2)

Where parameters $a \le b \le c \le d$ define the base corners and shoulder plateaus. Left-shoulder trapezoids handle boundary minimums ($a = b$), and right-shoulder trapezoids handle asymptotic maximums ($c = d$).

Fig 2. Input Variable 1: Age Fuzzification Membership Functions across the universe $[18, 85]$ years. Piecewise curves define Young ($18–36$y, left shoulder), Middle-Aged ($28–56$y, triangular), and Senior ($48–80+$y, right shoulder). For an evaluated client at Age 38y, the function computes continuous memberships $\mu_{\text{Young}} = 0.00$, $\mu_{\text{Middle}} = 0.71$, and $\mu_{\text{Senior}} = 0.00$.

4.2. Rule Evaluation via Gödel Min T-Norm

The knowledge base contains 9 expert-curated fuzzy rules that map antecedents to consequence sets (Conservative, Moderate, Aggressive). Multi-antecedent conjunction is evaluated using the Gödel Min T-Norm:

$$\alpha_i = \min\left(\mu_{\text{Age}, i}, \mu_{\text{Horiz}, i}, \mu_{\text{Loss}, i}, \mu_{\text{Inc}, i}\right)$$
(3)
Fig 6. Mamdani Rule Base Evaluation: Consequent Class Firing Heights ($\alpha$) evaluated across the 9 Gödel Min rules for the client profile. Rule conjunction produces firing heights of $\alpha_{\text{Conservative}} = 0.40$, $\alpha_{\text{Moderate}} = 0.50$, and $\alpha_{\text{Aggressive}} = 0.00$.

Chapter 7: Implication, Aggregation & Centroid Defuzzification

4.3. Mamdani Min-Implication Clipping

In Mamdani inference, consequent fuzzy sets are clipped at the rule firing strength $\alpha_i$ using the minimum operator:

$$\mu_{C'_i}(y) = \min(\alpha_i, \mu_{C_i}(y))$$
(4)
Fig 7. Mamdani Min-Implication: Consequent Membership Function Truncation. The raw output sets (Conservative, Moderate, Aggressive) are clipped at their respective firing strengths ($\alpha_{\text{cons}}=0.40, \alpha_{\text{mod}}=0.50$), preserving qualitative fuzzy boundaries without step discontinuities.

4.4. S-Norm Aggregation & Centroid Defuzzification

Clipped consequent sets are unified across the universe of discourse $Y \in [0, 100]$ using the standard Maximum S-Norm operator:

$$\mu_{\text{Agg}}(y) = \max_{i=1 \dots 9}\left[\min\left(\alpha_i, \mu_{C_i}(y)\right)\right]$$

The crisp risk score $R^* \in [0, 100]$ is defuzzified using the numerical Center of Gravity (Centroid) method:

$$R^* = \frac{\int_{0}^{100} y \cdot \mu_{\text{Agg}}(y)\, dy}{\int_{0}^{100} \mu_{\text{Agg}}(y)\, dy}$$
(5)
Fig 8. S-Norm Aggregation & Center of Gravity (Centroid) Defuzzification. The shaded area represents the unified aggregated fuzzy set $\mu_{\text{Agg}}(y)$. The vertical red line indicates the exact geometric center of mass ($R^* = 50.0 / 100$), classifying the client definitively into the Moderate Risk Category.

Chapter 8: Tier 2 – Deterministic Forward-Chaining Rule Engine

5.1. Translating Risk Scores to Baseline Asset Allocation

Once $R^*$ is defuzzified, the engine establishes the baseline target equity exposure:

$$\text{Target Equity } \% = 15.0\% + \left(\frac{R^*}{100.0}\right) \times 65.0\%$$
(6)

Equation (6) establishes a conservative baseline: minimum equity exposure is capped at 15.0% (for $R^*=0$, ensuring inflation hedging even for ultra-conservative clients), while maximum equity exposure reaches 80.0% (for $R^*=100$, leaving 20.0% for sovereign debt and physical gold to cushion market crashes).

5.2. Priority-Queued Invariant Rule Execution

FinWise AI enforces five non-negotiable production rules scheduled via a descending priority queue ($P \in [70, 100]$):

Priority Rule Identifier Fiduciary Mandate & Action Invariant
P = 100 RULE_ALLOCATION_SUM_100 Enforces that all portfolio weights sum to exactly 100.0% ($\sum w_i = 100.0\%$). Clamps and normalizes weights. Sum = 100%
P = 90 RULE_EMERGENCY_FUND_DEFICIT Audits liquid reserves against 6 months of living expenses. Re-routes new monthly cashflows to liquid debt until emergency fund is fully capitalized. Liquidity First
P = 85 RULE_HIGH_INTEREST_AVALANCHE If client holds debt with APR $> 12.0\%$ (e.g., credit card debt, personal loans), equity allocation is suspended; cashflows are redirected to eliminate debt. Guaranteed ROI
P = 80 RULE_BUDGET_50_30_20 Audits monthly income against Elizabeth Warren's 50/30/20 framework: Needs ($\le 50\%$), Wants ($\le 30\%$), and Savings/Investments ($\ge 20\%$). Cashflow Solvency
P = 70 RULE_TAX_OPTIMIZATION_80C Identifies statutory tax-saving opportunities under Section 80C of the Indian Income Tax Act (ELSS mutual funds and NPS tier-1). Tax Efficiency
Fiduciary Guarantee: The priority-queued architecture guarantees that debt elimination ($P=85$) and emergency reserves ($P=90$) strictly precede equity exposure. A user with ₹5,00,000 in credit card debt at 42% APR will never be advised to speculate in equities, resolving a primary flaw observed in commercial chatbots.

Chapter 9: Tier 3 – Grounded Agentic AI & Explainability Layer

6.1. PydanticAI Agent Architecture

Tier 3 serves as the conversational interface between the client and the mathematical core. Rather than allowing an LLM to generate recommendations freely, FinWise AI uses PydanticAI to structure the agent as a strict, tool-calling state machine:

  • Tool Calling Protocol: The agent queries validated local tools (`get_client_portfolio`, `audit_emergency_fund`, `calculate_debt_avalanche`).
  • Live Macroeconomic Grounding: The agent retrieves live macroeconomic benchmarks via web search (e.g., current Reserve Bank of India Repo Rate at 6.50%, 10-Year Indian Government Sovereign Bond Yield at 6.84%) to contextualize debt fund yields and fixed deposit comparisons.
  • Zero-Hallucination Fallback Safeguard: If an external LLM endpoint fails or times out, FinWise AI automatically falls back to an internal deterministic response engine that synthesizes audit explanations directly from the session SQLite database with zero hallucination risk.

Chapter 17: Conclusion, Limitations & Future Work

11.1. Summary of Contributions

FinWise AI demonstrates a viable, robust paradigm for automated retail wealth management. By decoupling subjective human risk perception from deterministic statutory verification and conversational synthesis:

  • Continuous Mamdani fuzzy inference accurately quantifies client risk appetite without arbitrary boundary cliff-effects (98.61% Monotonicity Index).
  • Priority forward-chaining rules deterministically guarantee legal invariants ($\sum w_i = 100.0\%$, 6-month emergency cash buffer, and debt avalanche scheduling) across 50,000 Monte Carlo iterations.
  • A 17-year historical market backtest across Indian financial assets (2008–2024) proves that FinWise Moderate cuts portfolio volatility by 54% and cuts maximum drawdown from -51.80% down to -17.44% while outpacing 100% Nifty 50 equity in terminal wealth compounding.

11.2. Architectural Limitations

While FinWise AI resolves the fiduciary limitations of pure generative chatbots, certain constraints remain:

  1. Domestic Asset Universe: The current asset allocation universe is restricted to Indian domestic instruments (Nifty 50, CRISIL Bonds, Domestic Gold, and Liquid Cash). International geographical diversification (e.g., S&P 500 or global bonds) is not yet integrated.
  2. Tax-Loss Harvesting: The system models static 80C tax optimization but lacks dynamic daily capital gains tax-loss harvesting algorithms.
  3. Discrete Annual Rebalancing: Backtesting assumes annual calendar rebalancing; dynamic volatility-triggered threshold rebalancing has not yet been implemented.

11.3. Future Work

Future iterations of FinWise AI will incorporate Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) to fine-tune continuous membership curves against actual user trade execution behavior. We also plan to integrate multi-lingual voice control interfaces to support retail vernacular investors across Tier-2 and Tier-3 Indian cities, alongside multi-currency international ETF portfolio rebalancing.
