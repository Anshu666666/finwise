Project Abstract
For CSE311: Artificial Intelligence at IIIT Kottayam, our team developed FinWise AI, an explainable financial advisory system. In preliminary experiments, we observed that while standard Large Language Models (LLMs) can generate natural language responses, they frequently hallucinate asset allocation percentages that do not add up to 100%, recommend conflicting financial actions, and lack mathematical accountability. Conversely, textbook heuristics (such as the rigid "100 minus age" rule) introduce arbitrary boundary cliff-edges where a single birthday drastically alters asset allocations.

To address these limitations, we designed and implemented a modular AI pipeline:
  1. Mamdani Fuzzy Inference System (FIS): We implemented triangular and trapezoidal membership functions in Python across 4 continuous inputs (Age, Investment Horizon, Loss Tolerance, Income Stability) and Center-of-Gravity (Centroid) defuzzification to model human risk appetite smoothly.
  2. Priority-Based Forward-Chaining Rule Engine: We built an expert rule engine with prioritized conflict resolution to strictly enforce essential financial invariants ($\sum w_i = 100.0\%$, a mandatory 6-month liquid emergency fund, and high-interest debt payoff prioritization).
  3. Grounded Conversational Interface: A full-stack web application (FastAPI, SQLite, Vanilla JS) that connects the rule engine to an assistant that retrieves live macroeconomic rates (RBI Repo Rate at 6.50%, 10Y Indian G-Sec yield) with a deterministic fallback engine.
To evaluate our system, we conducted empirical tests: (1) a 1,296-point Cartesian grid test showing 98.61% monotonicity with zero gaps; (2) 50,000 Monte Carlo simulations confirming 100% preservation of safety invariants; and (3) an empirical 17-year historical market backtest (2008–2024) using authentic Indian market data (Nifty 50, CRISIL Composite Bonds, Physical Gold, and Repo Rate). FinWise Moderate achieved a 10.18% CAGR with 12.19% volatility (Sharpe ratio 0.302 vs 0.123 for pure Nifty 50), reducing the 2008 Lehman crisis maximum drawdown from -51.80% down to -17.44%.

Chapter 1: Introduction & Problem Statement

1.1. Motivation for the AI Project

In personal finance, selecting an appropriate asset allocation (the split between equities, debt, gold, and cash) is the single biggest determinant of long-term investment success. However, retail investors in India face a significant guidance gap: certified financial planners are expensive, while online advice is either overly generic or driven by sales commissions.

With recent advances in generative AI, chatbots have become a popular tool for automated advice [1], [2]. In our initial experiments, we tested prompting standard LLMs directly for personal financial planning advice. We discovered three critical technical shortcomings:

  • Arithmetic Inconsistency: Because LLMs predict tokens probabilistically, they often output asset allocation percentages that do not add up to 100% (e.g., suggesting 60% equity, 30% debt, 15% gold, and 5% cash), or produce negative numbers when constrained.
  • Missing Financial Safety Rules: LLMs lack a deterministic mechanism to enforce essential safety constraints. For example, a chatbot might advise an individual with high-interest credit card debt (costing 40% annually) to invest in volatile small-cap stocks, violating basic financial common sense.
  • Hallucinated Facts & Rates: General LLMs lack accurate, real-time awareness of current macroeconomic data such as the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) repo rate [8] or government treasury bond yields, leading to misleading yield projections.

1.2. Shortcomings of Existing Heuristic Systems

On the other hand, traditional automated financial tools rely on rigid, discrete rules like the popular "100 minus age" formula. These introduce artificial "cliff-edge" discontinuities:

  • An investor celebrating their 36th birthday is abruptly moved into a different risk tier, cutting equity exposure by 10% overnight, even though their real-world financial situation did not change.
  • Traditional scoring schemes fail to account for multi-dimensional factors simultaneously, such as separating psychological loss tolerance from objective income stability.

1.3. Project Goal & AI Approach

The goal of this project was to build FinWise AI by combining two fundamental AI paradigms covered in our syllabus:

  1. Fuzzy Logic (Knowledge Representation): Continuous membership functions that model human risk perception without discrete boundary cliffs.
  2. Expert Systems (Automated Reasoning): A forward-chaining rule engine with priority-based conflict resolution that deterministically guarantees safety constraints.
  3. Full-Stack Prototype: A working web interface backed by FastAPI and SQLite where conversational explanations are strictly grounded in certified rule traces.

Chapter 2: Literature Review & Core AI Concepts

2.1. Modern Portfolio Theory & Risk-Return Metrics

Harry Markowitz (1952) [3] demonstrated that investors can minimize portfolio variance for a given expected return by holding a diversified basket of uncorrelated assets. The portfolio variance ($\sigma_p^2$) is computed as:

$$\sigma_p^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n w_i^2 \sigma_i^2 + 2 \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{j < i} w_i w_j \text{Cov}(R_i, R_j)$$

William Sharpe (1964) [4] introduced the Sharpe Ratio ($S_p = (R_p - R_f) / \sigma_p$) to evaluate excess return per unit of total risk against a risk-free benchmark ($R_f$). These classical formulas form the basis of our historical backtesting benchmarks.

2.2. Fuzzy Set Theory & Mamdani Inference

Lotfi Zadeh (1965) [5] introduced fuzzy set theory to represent human concepts with continuous truth degrees $\mu \in [0, 1]$ rather than binary Boolean logic $\{0, 1\}$. In finance, terms like "young investor", "moderate horizon", and "stable income" have gradual boundaries that are naturally modeled by fuzzy sets.

Mamdani and Assilian (1975) [6] developed the Mamdani fuzzy inference system (FIS). As formulated in Course PPT: 3.5 Fuzzy Inference - Mamdani Style.pptx [7], by combining rule antecedents using the Gödel Min T-norm ($\alpha_i = \min_j \mu_{ij}$), clipping output sets via min-implication, and defuzzifying through Center-of-Gravity (Centroid), the Mamdani engine produces a smooth, continuous mapping from input parameters to risk scores without sudden jumps.

2.3. Production Rules & Forward Chaining

Expert systems represent domain knowledge as production rules of the form $\text{IF } \langle\text{condition}\rangle \text{ THEN } \langle\text{action}\rangle$. In our design, we implement forward-chaining deduction with a prioritized conflict resolution strategy ($P = 100 \dots 70$). This guarantees that hard safety rules (such as maintaining a 6-month emergency reserve) are always verified before discretionary equity allocations are made.

Course Syllabus Alignment: This architecture demonstrates two core topics from our AI course: Knowledge Representation using Fuzzy Logic (Module 3) and Automated Reasoning using Forward Chaining Expert Systems (Module 4).

Chapter 4: Tier 1 – Continuous Mamdani Fuzzy Inference Engine

4.1. Input Fuzzification: Age & Investment Horizon

We model 4 continuous input variables: Age ($x_{\text{age}} \in [18, 85]$), Investment Horizon ($x_{\text{horiz}} \in [0, 30]$ yrs), Loss Tolerance ($x_{\text{loss}} \in [0, 100]$), and Income Stability ($x_{\text{inc}} \in [0, 100]$).

In accordance with the continuous membership definitions presented in Course PPT: 3.5 Fuzzy Inference - Mamdani Style.pptx [7], we implemented standard piecewise triangular ($\mu_{\text{tri}}$) and trapezoidal ($\mu_{\text{trap}}$) functions from scratch in Python:

$$\mu_{\text{tri}}(x; a, b, c) = \max\left(0, \min\left(\frac{x-a}{b-a}, \frac{c-x}{c-b}\right)\right)$$
(1)
$$\mu_{\text{trap}}(x; a, b, c, d) = \max\left(0, \min\left(\frac{x-a}{b-a}, 1, \frac{d-x}{d-c}\right)\right)$$
(2)
Fig 2. Input Variable 1: Age Fuzzification ($[18, 85]$ yrs). Partitioned into Young (trapezoid $[18, 18, 28, 38]$), Middle-Aged (triangular $[30, 42, 55]$), and Senior (trapezoid $[48, 60, 85, 85]$). For a 38-year-old client, $\mu_{\text{Middle}} = 0.71$.
Fig 3. Input Variable 2: Investment Horizon Fuzzification ($[0, 30]$ yrs). Partitioned into Short ($< 3$y), Medium ($3–12$y), and Long ($> 8$y). For an 8-year horizon, $\mu_{\text{Medium}} = 0.57$.

4.2. Input Fuzzification: Loss Tolerance & Income Stability

In financial planning, an investor's risk profile depends on both psychological readiness (willingness to accept volatility) and financial stability (ability to withstand financial shocks without liquidating assets). We model these using two normalized inputs $[0, 100]$ partitioned into linguistic fuzzy sets as defined in Course PPT: 3.5 Fuzzy Inference - Mamdani Style.pptx [7]:

Fig 4. Input Variable 3: Psychological Loss Tolerance ($[0, 100]$). Evaluates client emotional comfort with volatility into Low ($[0, 0, 20, 40]$), Medium ($[25, 50, 75]$), and High ($[60, 80, 100, 100]$). For input = 50, $\mu_{\text{Medium}} = 1.00$.
Fig 5. Input Variable 4: Income Stability ($[0, 100]$). Classifies career and cashflow predictability into Unstable ($[0, 0, 25, 45]$), Moderate ($[30, 55, 80]$), and Stable ($[65, 85, 100, 100]$). For input = 85, $\mu_{\text{Stable}} = 1.00$.

By separating psychological tolerance from income capacity, our fuzzy model avoids treating a high-income young investor with low loss tolerance the same as a low-income retiree.

Chapter 4: Tier 1 – Mamdani Fuzzy Inference Engine (Cont.)

4.3. Gödel Min Conjunction, Implication & Centroid Defuzzification

Our rule base consists of 9 linguistic IF-THEN rules designed around standard financial planning heuristics. In accordance with the inference pipeline in Course PPT: 3.5 Fuzzy Inference - Mamdani Style.pptx [7], rule antecedents are evaluated using the Gödel Min T-Norm:

$$\alpha_i = \min\left(\mu_{\text{Age}, i}, \mu_{\text{Horiz}, i}, \mu_{\text{Loss}, i}, \mu_{\text{Inc}, i}\right)$$
(3)
Fig 6. Rule Firing Strengths: Active firing heights $\alpha_{\text{Conservative}} = 0.40$, $\alpha_{\text{Moderate}} = 0.50$, and $\alpha_{\text{Aggressive}} = 0.00$.

In Mamdani min-implication, consequent fuzzy sets are truncated by their firing height: $\mu_{C'_i}(y) = \min(\alpha_i, \mu_{C_i}(y))$. The clipped sets are aggregated using the Max S-Norm $\mu_{\text{Agg}}(y) = \max_i[\min(\alpha_i, \mu_{C_i}(y))]$ and defuzzified via numerical Center-of-Gravity (Centroid) as specified in Course PPT: 3.5 Fuzzy Inference - Mamdani Style.pptx [7]:

$$R^* = \frac{\int_{0}^{100} y \cdot \mu_{\text{Agg}}(y)\, dy}{\int_{0}^{100} \mu_{\text{Agg}}(y)\, dy}$$
(4)
Fig 7. Mamdani Min-Implication: Consequent sets clipped at $\alpha_{\text{cons}}=0.40$ and $\alpha_{\text{mod}}=0.50$.
Fig 8. Center of Gravity Defuzzification: Computes the continuous balance line $R^* = 50.0 / 100$ (Moderate Risk).

Chapter 5: Tier 2 – Priority Forward-Chaining Rule Engine

5.1. Baseline Target Equity Formula

Once the continuous crisp risk score $R^* \in [0, 100]$ is computed by Tier 1, the forward-chaining engine calculates the baseline target equity allocation:

$$\text{Target Equity } \% = 15.0\% + \left(\frac{R^*}{100.0}\right) \times 65.0\%$$
(5)

For our test profile ($R^* = 50.0$), baseline equity equals $15\% + 32.5\% = 47.5\%$. The remaining $52.5\%$ is systematically distributed across debt ($37.5\%$), domestic gold ($10.0\%$), and liquid cash ($5.0\%$).

5.2. Priority Queue Conflict Resolution

To prevent rule conflicts and enforce prudential safety standards aligning with SEBI advisory norms [9], our forward-chaining engine uses a priority-ordered agenda ($P = 100 \dots 70$):

Priority Rule Identifier Action & Safety Guarantee
P = 100 RULE_ALLOCATION_SUM_100 Enforces $\sum w_i = 100.0\%$. Normalizes all weights deterministically.
P = 90 RULE_EMERGENCY_FUND_DEFICIT Requires 6 months of expenses in cash before equity investing is permitted.
P = 85 RULE_HIGH_INTEREST_AVALANCHE If client holds debt $> 12\%$ APR (credit cards), equity is paused for debt payoff.
P = 80 RULE_BUDGET_50_30_20 Audits monthly cashflow: Needs $\le 50\%$, Wants $\le 30\%$, Savings $\ge 20\%$.
P = 70 RULE_TAX_OPTIMIZATION_80C Allocates up to ₹1.5L statutory deduction into tax-saving ELSS and NPS.
Engineering Rationale: By decoupling the continuous risk score (Tier 1) from hard rule invariants (Tier 2), the system guarantees that high-interest debt repayment strictly overrides stock market speculation.
