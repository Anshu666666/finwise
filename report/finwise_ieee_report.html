Abstract—
Mutual fund and asset allocation chatbots have emerged as scalable tools for democratizing personal wealth advisory. However, pure generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) and Large Language Models (LLMs) suffer from severe fiduciary limitations: probabilistic hallucinations, inability to guarantee mathematical invariants (such as allocations summing to 100%), and non-deterministic divergence across client interactions. Conversely, legacy robo-advisors rely on rigid step-function scoring algorithms that introduce arbitrary boundary cliff-effects and fail to capture multi-dimensional risk appetite. To resolve this challenge, we developed FinWise AI, an explainable neuro-symbolic financial advisory system. The system integrates: (1) a continuous Mamdani Fuzzy Inference System (FIS) that utilizes continuous triangular and trapezoidal membership functions with numerical Center-of-Gravity (Centroid) defuzzification to determine risk tolerance without artificial step-jumps; (2) a priority-queued Forward-Chaining Expert Rule Engine guaranteeing non-negotiable financial invariants (100% allocation balance, 6-month emergency reserves, and debt avalanche scheduling); and (3) a Tool-Augmented Agentic Layer that grounds generative natural language responses in verified live macroeconomic indicators (RBI repo rates, sovereign bond yields). The architecture was evaluated across a 1,296-point 4D state-space grid (98.61% Monotonicity Index), a 50,000-profile Monte Carlo stress test (100% invariant preservation), a 150-case Certified Financial Planner (CFP) benchmark comparison (97.33% concordance), and an empirical 17-year historical market backtest (2008–2024). Over the 17-year period, FinWise Moderate delivered a Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) of 10.18% with an annualized volatility of 12.19% and a Sharpe Ratio of 0.302 (vs 0.123 for pure Nifty 50 equity), cutting the 2008 Lehman collapse maximum drawdown from -51.80% down to -17.44%.
Index Terms—Fuzzy Inference Systems, Mamdani FIS, Expert Systems, Neuro-Symbolic AI, Portfolio Optimization, Robo-Advisory, Financial Machine Learning, Backtesting.

1. Introduction

The wealth management industry has witnessed substantial transformation through automated robo-advisory and conversational financial interfaces [1], [2]. While natural language processing enables conversational accessibility for retail investors [6], deploying autonomous Large Language Models (LLMs) in fiduciary environments presents grave systemic risks [12].

Recent audits demonstrate that black-box language models frequently hallucinate numerical returns, recommend negative asset weights, or violate basic balance constraints [12], [13]. Financial regulatory frameworks, such as the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) Investment Advisers Regulations [19] and the US SEC Fiduciary Standard, legally require that financial advice be explainable, consistent, and provably aligned with client risk capacity.

Existing commercial robo-advisors attempt to circumvent this issue by employing rigid scoring heuristics (e.g., the naive "100 minus age" rule or discrete questionnaire thresholds). These models introduce severe step-function discontinuities ("cliffs"): an investor turning 36 years old may have their equity exposure cut abruptly by 10% overnight, regardless of their market horizon, income stability, or personal loss tolerance.

To overcome both the hallucinations of LLMs and the rigidity of static scoring heuristics, we present FinWise AI. By decoupling continuous risk perception (handled by Mamdani Fuzzy Inference) and regulatory fiduciary constraints (handled by deterministic forward-chaining rules) from natural language communication (handled by a tool-augmented agent), FinWise AI guarantees both absolute mathematical safety and rich, human-like interaction.

2. Related Works

Modern Portfolio Theory (MPT), formulated by Markowitz [3] and expanded by Sharpe [4], forms the mathematical bedrock of asset diversification. However, classical mean-variance optimization requires precise return and covariance estimates, which are notoriously unstable in volatile markets. Real-world fiduciary guidelines (CFP Board [18]) emphasize lifecycle-stage asset allocation, prioritizing emergency liquidity and debt payoff over unconstrained volatility minimization.

Fuzzy set theory, introduced by Zadeh [14], and linguistic fuzzy modeling, pioneered by Mamdani and Assilian [15], provide an effective mathematical framework for modeling continuous human risk tolerance [5], [16]. By mapping subjective investor inputs onto overlapping continuous linguistic curves, Mamdani FIS eliminates the boundary cliff-effects characteristic of discrete risk matrices.

Production expert systems [8], [9] enforce invariant safety constraints through forward-chaining priority queues. Recent agentic frameworks (Yao et al., ReAct [10]) demonstrate that restricting generative models to contextual tool synthesis while delegating math to deterministic systems mitigates hallucination. Recent usability studies by Suchonwanich et al. [1] and comparative evaluations by Oehler and Horn [12] emphasize that successful automated advisors must combine conversational empathy with provably rigorous quantitative backtesting.

3.1. Tier 1: Continuous Mamdani Fuzzy Inference System

The risk assessment engine maps four continuous inputs into linguistic variables: Age ($x_{\text{age}} \in [18, 85]$), Horizon ($x_{\text{horiz}} \in [0, 30]$ yrs), Loss Tolerance ($x_{\text{loss}} \in [0, 100]$), and Income Stability ($x_{\text{inc}} \in [0, 100]$).

Piecewise triangular and trapezoidal membership functions map crisp inputs to continuous truth values $\mu \in [0, 1]$:

μtri(x; a, b, c) = max(0, min((x-a)/(b-a), (c-x)/(c-b)))
(1)
μtrap(x; a, b, c, d) = max(0, min((x-a)/(b-a), 1, (d-x)/(d-c)))
(2)

Multi-antecedent conjunction across 9 deterministic rules evaluates the Gödel Min T-Norm:

αi = min(μAge,i, μHoriz,i, μLoss,i, μInc,i)
(3)

Output consequent risk sets are clipped at firing strength $\alpha_i$ and unified via S-Norm maximum aggregation:

μAgg(y) = maxi=1..9 [ min(αi, μCi(y)) ]
(4)

The crisp risk score $R^* \in [0, 100]$ is defuzzified via numerical Center of Gravity (Centroid):

R* = ∫ y · μAgg(y) dy / ∫ μAgg(y) dy
(5)
Fig 2. 4-Stage Continuous Mamdani Fuzzy Inference System workflow generated via pure Matplotlib for an evaluated profile (Age 38, Horizon 8y, Loss Tolerance 45, Income Stability 80), showing piecewise triangular/trapezoidal input fuzzification, Min implication clipping, S-Norm aggregation, and numerical Centroid defuzzification ($R^* = 50.0$).

3.2. Tier 2: Deterministic Forward-Chaining Rule Engine

Once $R^*$ is computed, the engine calculates the baseline target equity exposure:

Equity % = 15.0% + (R* / 100.0) × 65.0%
(6)

A priority queue ($P = 100 \dots 70$) enforces strict fiduciary invariants:

  • RULE_ASSET_ALLOCATION_SUM_100 ($P=100$): Enforces $\sum w_i = 100.0\%$ balance.
  • RULE_EMERGENCY_FUND_DEFICIT ($P=90$): Requires 6 months of living expenses in liquid cash before stock allocation.
  • RULE_HIGH_INTEREST_DEBT_AVALANCHE ($P=85$): Prioritizes debt payoff over market speculation if interest exceeds 12% APR.
  • RULE_BUDGET_50_30_20 ($P=80$): Balances Needs ($\le 50\%$), Wants ($\le 30\%$), and Investments ($\ge 20\%$).
  • RULE_TAX_OPTIMIZATION_80C ($P=70$): Optimizes statutory ELSS and NPS deductions.

3.3. Tier 3: Grounded Agentic AI and Conversational Engine

The agent layer queries live macroeconomic indicators (RBI Repo Rate 6.50%, 10Y Sovereign G-Sec 6.84%) to ground natural language reports. If external LLM endpoints experience latency, a built-in deterministic fallback engine answers questions about equity math, emergency deficits, and debt repayment using exact session calculations.

4.3. Performance Comparison with Existing Systems

As is clear from the empirical comparison, the proposed neuro-symbolic framework has decisive advantages over traditional Linear Regression and standalone Random Forest methods. Linear regression, being simpler and more understandable, cannot deal with complex, non-linear relationships or adapt to multi-asset market dynamics. By contrast, while Random Forest handles non-linearities, it operates as a statistical black box lacking mathematical safety guarantees, frequently outputting negative asset weights or allocations exceeding 100% when exposed to out-of-distribution inputs.

In contrast, FinWise AI guarantees both continuous mathematical interpretability (via Mamdani fuzzy membership) and provable fiduciary safety (via forward-chaining invariants). Table 3 details the empirical backtesting outcomes against traditional benchmarks over 17 years (2008–2024).

The backtesting yields three critical empirical findings:

  1. Wealth Compounding Outperformance: FinWise Moderate generated ₹5,19,730 (10.18% CAGR) vs ₹4,86,548 for 100% Nifty 50 equity, benefiting from periodic rebalancing into undervalued assets.
  2. Risk Reduction by 54%: FinWise Moderate cut annualized portfolio volatility from 26.49% down to 12.19% and achieved a Sharpe Ratio of 0.302—nearly 2.5× higher than pure equity (0.123).
  3. Catastrophic Drawdown Protection: In the 2008 Lehman collapse, 100% Nifty 50 suffered a catastrophic -51.80% drawdown. FinWise Moderate suffered only a -17.44% decline, buffered by domestic gold (+29.5%) and sovereign bonds (+10.2%).

5. Conclusion And Future Work

Mutual fund investments have evolved due to the creation of AI-based financial advisory systems integrating machine learning (ML), artificial intelligence (AI), and natural language processing (NLP) to give personalized, data-driven advice [1], [6]. With minimal human intervention, the proposed Mutual Fund Chatbot enhances choice-making by analyzing client risk appetite, understanding financial goals, and generating real-time insights [7], [11]. The chatbot provides correct portfolio suggestions through the use of neuro-symbolic modeling, making investing more efficient, accessible, and mathematically safe [12], [13].

Future studies will utilize predictive modeling, deep learning, and sentiment analysis from social media and financial news to enhance the analytical capabilities of the chatbot and make more accurate market predictions [10], [16]. Increased accessibility will come in the form of extended multi-modal interaction, such as voice control and linguistic support. Cybersecurity, legal compliance (SEBI [19]), and ethical AI practices will all need to be enhanced to safeguard data privacy and credibility [13]. Blockchain integration will also be explored to enhance security and transparency.

Reinforcement learning will allow the chatbot to continuously adapt to user behaviour and changes in the market. Expert advice and AI-driven insights will also be coupled with real-time collaboration with financial experts. As artificial intelligence and financial technology continue to advance, the system will evolve into a strong digital guidance tool that links financial wisdom with accessible investment vehicles [14], [15].
